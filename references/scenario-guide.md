# 积极育儿场景实操指南

Use this support guide when the user asks for more concrete examples than `scenario-template.md` provides. Keep examples role-neutral unless the user has named a role. Prefer "你", "照护者", "孩子", "老师", "祖辈", or the user's own role words.

## Style rules

- Do not force a parent role or intimate child nickname.
- Do not add decorative emoji.
- Keep examples warm but usable under stress.
- For teachers, use classroom language and group-routine constraints.
- For grandparents, nannies, partners, or other caregivers, keep advice within the authority they actually have.

## Bedtime Delay

### Prepare the routine

Create a simple visual bedtime routine with the child:

```text
wash / diaper or toilet -> teeth -> agreed stories -> short connection -> lights out -> sleep
```

Useful details:

- Put bathroom or diaper steps before stories so they are not used as repeated delay loops.
- Agree on the number of stories before starting.
- Let the child help choose or draw the routine.
- During the routine, point to the chart instead of repeating long reminders.

### In-the-moment script

```text
"今天的故事时间到了。你可以选牵手躺下，或者抱一下再躺下。"
```

If the child cries:

```text
"你还想继续，我知道。故事结束了。我在这里陪你安静一会儿。"
```

If the caregiver needs a pause:

```text
"我现在需要冷静一分钟。你在这里是安全的，我马上回来。"
```

### Keep the boundary

- Use the same short sentence.
- Do not add new stories, snacks, or negotiations after the boundary.
- If sleep disruption is severe, persistent, or linked with breathing concerns, use `safety-triage.md` and recommend professional input.

## Food Throwing

### Check before correcting

Food throwing may come from fullness, texture discomfort, boredom, exploration, fatigue, or a big adult reaction. Do not reduce it to one motive.

### In-the-moment script

```text
"食物是吃的，不是扔的。继续扔，我会把碗收走，表示这餐结束。"
```

If throwing continues:

```text
"看来这餐结束了。我们先下餐椅。"
```

Then act calmly: remove the food and end the meal. Avoid threats, shame, or bargaining for "two more bites."

### Repair and prevention

- After calm returns, invite simple cleanup as contribution, not punishment.
- Offer smaller portions.
- Notice whether the child is full, tired, overstimulated, or reacting to adult intensity.
- Let the child make one small acceptable choice before meals.
